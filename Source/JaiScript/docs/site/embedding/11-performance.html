Performance Notes​

Honest numbers, with their sources. The definitive report is docs/PERFORMANCE.md (2026-07 capstone: min-of-5 both backends, quiet machine, full suite green at the measured commit); the benchmark suites live in source/tests/performance/ (Release BENCHMARKS config, -DJAISCRIPT_ENABLE_BENCHMARKS=ON): benchmark_suite.cpp ("Performance Benchmarks"), squirrel_comparison.cpp, sol2_comparison.cpp (Lua 5.4), and chaiscript_comparison.cpp (headers restored for live measurement). Two standing caveats from the project's own docs: the harness reports integer µs/iteration with ±50% run-to-run variance (compare min-of-N, don't trust ±1 µs deltas), and all comparative numbers below are from the VM-perf branch, July 2026, MSVC x64 Release.

Single-operation costs (order-of-magnitude intuition)​

From docs/PERFORMANCE.md (2026-07 capstone, interp / VM):

operationcost
single arithmetic op<1 µs both backends
function call~2 µs interp / ~1 µs VM (free call in a tight loop: 0.9 / 0.44 µs)
class creation~15 µs interp / ~14 µs VM
method invocation~12 µs interp / ~9 µs VM (per-call in a tight loop: ~2.4 µs either backend)
hot loop, per iteration~47 ns (VM, op_compound_fused: 1000-iteration loop ≈ 47 µs; band 44–48 held at HEAD)
engine creation~73–77 µs (+ lazy backend init on first execute; ~90 µs all-in)
stdlib registration~311 µs

Warm dispatch costs are in the Backends chapter (docs/execution_mode_metrics.md): a warm repeated tiny script is ~0.6 µs on the VM (~0.38 µs via jaibite), 1.7–1.8× faster than the interpreter.

  • vs Squirrel (squirrel_comparison.cpp): the VM wins 12 of 19 head-to-head benchmarks, loses 6, ties 1 (12W/6L/1T — the previously published 13th win, the counted for-loop, reads 10 vs 10 at HEAD: a tie at harness resolution). Wins everything statement-, container-, string- and object-shaped; the losses are deep recursion and the raw-loop family. Squirrel remains "the beatable one".
  • vs Lua 5.4 via sol2 (sol2_comparison.cpp): an honest split, 7W/10L/2T — JaiScript wins or ties trivial statements, containers, range-for and null-checks; Lua wins the loop family (~2–8×) and above all call-dense recursion: ~9× on fib(15) (697 vs 75 µs) up to ~14× on locals-heavy deep recursion. Lua is "the tough yardstick".
  • vs ChaiScript (chaiscript_comparison.cpp, measured live 2026-07 after retuning its algorithm rows in ChaiScript's favor): a clean sweep — 29W/0L across every live row, typically 8–20× (function calls 13×, method dispatch 13×, fib(6) ~20×), 1.6× at the narrowest (C++-bound BST) and ~50× at the widest (script BST, historical figure).

What's fast (and why)​

The VM-perf pass closed the interpreter/VM gap and then some; the machinery behind the numbers (all landed, both backends green):

  • Loops: counted-for compiles to dedicated opcodes (op_cfor_prep/back) with a generic bytecode fallback for exotic cases; scope elision skips per-iteration environment allocation (the original 7.7× hot-loop deficit was exactly this); environment-lookup inline caches kill the per-iteration hash lookups.
  • Fused superinstructions: op_binary_fused, op_fused_cmp_jump, and op_compound_fused collapse the common x += f * 2.0 / compare-and-branch shapes — the compound fusion alone took the comparison hot loop from 118 to 45 µs (−62%).
  • In-loop call frames: script calls no longer recurse natively — call records are pooled and pushed/popped inside the run loop; method calls dispatch flattened, with zero-copy argument binding from the stack window.
  • Thin values: script_value is 32 bytes (was 56), static-assert-gated, two per cache line — measured as layout/copy headroom rather than a benchmark step, and documented that honestly (docs/thin_value_rebaseline.md: all 44 benchmarks within noise at integer-µs resolution).
  • Cheap safety: the checked-overflow policy is branch-free on the wrap build and intrinsic-based (_mul128) on the checked build; the budget clock samples every 1024 ticks — neither shows up in hot-path measurements.
  • Type-ladder dividends: typed (int) and inferred (auto) locals ride slot-based storage and typed fast paths; var stays fully dynamic and costs accordingly.

What's slower (and the plan)​

  • Call-dense recursion is the one structural gap vs Lua (2026-07 capstone: ~9× on fib(15), ~14× on the locals-heavy depth-15 recursion). The project's diagnosis (VM-perf branch notes): opcode counts are at parity with Lua (~7 ops per fib call); the cost is per-call frame setup — structural, not micro. The committed fix is the flat-stack / register-window VM rewrite — docs/why_jaiscript.md describes it as "on the roadmap, not a hope"; Squirrel's same-architecture calls (4× faster, not 9×) demonstrate the headroom. A second, cheaper roadmap item from the capstone: the comparison suites' hot loop (sum += i, single-operand RHS) misses op_compound_fused and runs 88 µs where the fused shape runs 47 — extending fusion flips a Squirrel loss and halves the visible Lua loop gap. Until then: game scripts that are loop- and method-shaped see a small-to-none gap; avoid deep naive recursion in per-frame hot paths.
  • Value semantics copies: passing a big structure by value deep-copies it. The idiom is & parameters for anything heavy — the by-ref BST benchmark runs ~3× closer to Lua than the by-value variant (the remainder is the same call-cost story; the by-value number is kept in the suite as the honest feature-cost figure, labeled [naive by-value]). The full measured three-way (value vs & vs shared_ptr, both backends) plus decision guidance is in docs/aliasing_costs.md and the Value Semantics chapter.
  • Class-heavy scripts: per-execute class redefinition/hot-reload bookkeeping dominates medium class scripts (~250 µs for the 30-line metric script) equally on both backends — batch class definitions once, don't re-execute them per frame.

Measuring yourself​

# Release BENCHMARKS build, then:
jaiscript_tests.exe "Performance Benchmarks" --verbose [--backend=vm]
jaiscript_tests.exe "Squirrel Performance Comparison" --verbose
jaiscript_tests.exe "Lua (sol2) Performance Comparison" --verbose   # needs HAVE_SOL2/lua checkout

Run min-of-3 and compare medians; the integer-µs harness cannot resolve sub-µs changes (use a dedicated ns micro-bench for those — Source/JaiScript/CLAUDE.md). For end-to-end embedding costs (engine create, parse, compile, warm dispatch, crossover points) the methodology and full tables are in docs/execution_mode_metrics.md, reproduced in the Backends chapter.
